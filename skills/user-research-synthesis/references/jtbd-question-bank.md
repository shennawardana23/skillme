# JTBD "Switch" interview — extended question bank

Read this when the four-timeframe summary in SKILL.md isn't enough detail
to actually run the interview. Use these as prompts to adapt in the
interviewee's own words, not a script to read verbatim — a script produces
stilted, guarded answers.

## Opening (anchor the specific event)

- "Tell me about the day you decided to [switch to X / cancel Y / buy Z]."
- "Before we go further — roughly when did that happen? What else was
  going on in your work/life around that time?" (Anchoring to a real
  calendar period makes later recall more concrete and checkable.)

## First thought (the struggling moment)

- "What was happening right before the idea of a different solution first
  crossed your mind?"
- "Was there a specific moment something broke, changed, or became newly
  painful — or did it build up gradually?"
- "What were you using before, and what was 'good enough' about it up
  until that point?"

## Passive looking

- "Between that first thought and when you actually started comparing
  options, how much time passed?"
- "During that gap, what kept the idea alive — did anyone mention
  something, did you see an ad, read a review, notice a competitor use
  it?"
- "What would have had to happen for you to keep ignoring the idea
  entirely?"

## Active looking / deciding

- "What finally pushed you from just thinking about it to actually
  looking for alternatives?"
- "Walk me through what you compared it to. What made it onto your
  shortlist, and what got ruled out early?"
- "Who else was involved in this decision? What did they care about that
  you didn't?"
- "What almost stopped you from switching? What were you worried you'd
  lose or get wrong?"
- "What made you finally decide — was there a specific moment or
  conversation that tipped it?"

## Consuming / onboarding

- "What happened in the first few days after you started using it?"
- "Was there a point where you doubted the decision? What got you past
  that?"
- "Did you tell anyone else about the switch — what did you say?"

## Cross-cutting probes (use throughout)

- "What were you feeling at that point?" (emotional state, not just facts)
- "What would your [boss / team / partner] have said about that choice?"
  (social context)
- "What was the worst-case outcome you were picturing?" (surfaces anxiety)
- "What made the old way still appealing, even a little?" (surfaces habit)

## Anti-patterns to avoid mid-interview

- Do not ask "what features would you want us to build" — that's a
  different exercise and collapses the interview into ordinary
  feature-request gathering.
- Do not accept "I just wanted something better" without following up —
  push for the specific trigger event and specific alternative considered.
- Do not let the interviewee jump straight to "consuming" — the
  first-thought and passive-looking stages are usually where the real
  causal story lives, and they're the stages people skip past unprompted.
